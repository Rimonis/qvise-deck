The Problem
The Forgetting Crisis
Students work hard.
Memory fails them.
70%
of new information forgotten within 24 hours of learning it
1
Re-reading feels productive. It isn't.
Most students highlight, re-read, and hope for the best. Research shows this is one of the least effective study strategies — yet it's the default because nothing better is easy enough to replace it.
2
Making flashcards takes longer than studying them
Students spend 3–4 hours creating cards for a single chapter — time stolen from actual learning. The process is repetitive, draining, and completely automatable by AI.
3
Spaced repetition works — but every app treats you like a machine
Anki, RemNote, and others schedule cards with scientific precision. But they assume you're available every single day. The algorithm doesn't know you have a lab on Tuesday, a part-time job on Thursday, or an exam in two days.
4
Miss one day. Come back to 200 overdue cards. Quit.
This is how most students abandon spaced repetition. Not because the science fails — but because the app does. Reviews pile up, guilt builds, and the tool designed to help becomes a source of stress.
The Solution
Introducing Qvise
Your notes. Transformed. Remembered.
Qvise generates flashcards from a student's own content, groups them by lesson, and schedules reviews around the student's real-life availability — using FSRS accuracy with a busyness-adaptive layer no other app has.
AI Generation
Upload any notes, PDF, or lecture slide. Gemini AI reads your content and writes targeted Q&A flashcards — grouped by lesson, not scattered randomly. Your source material stays attached to every deck.
"Stop making cards. Start learning."
Busyness-Adaptive Scheduling
Tell Qvise how busy your week is. The FSRS engine calculates optimal review timing per card — then our adaptive layer redistributes your load across your available days. Busy Monday? Your cards shift to Tuesday. No guilt. No backlog. No overdue pile.
"Your schedule shapes your study — not the other way around."
Lesson-Based Review
Other apps scatter your cards randomly across subjects. Qvise groups reviews by source — so your Thursday session is "Chapter 7: Cardiac Physiology," not 40 cards from 12 different topics. Study sessions that match how your brain actually organises knowledge.
"Review by lesson. Not by random queue."
