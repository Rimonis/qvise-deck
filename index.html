The Problem
The Forgetting Crisis
Students work hard.
Memory fails them.
70%
of new information forgotten within 24 hours of learning it
1
Re-reading feels productive. It isn't.
Highlighting and re-reading feel productive but are among the least effective strategies known. It's the default only because nothing better is frictionless enough to replace it.
2
Making flashcards takes longer than studying them
3–4 hours to card a single chapter — time stolen from actual learning, for a process AI can do in seconds.
3
Spaced repetition works — but every app treats you like a machine
Anki and RemNote schedule with precision — but assume you're free every day. The algorithm has no idea about your Tuesday lab, Thursday job, or Friday exam.
4
Miss one day. Come back to 200 overdue cards. Quit.
Reviews pile up, guilt builds, and the tool meant to help becomes a source of stress. Most students quit not because the science fails — but because the app does.
The Solution
Introducing Qvise
Your notes. Transformed. Remembered.
Upload your content. Qvise generates flashcards, groups them by lesson, and schedules reviews around your actual week — FSRS precision, adapted to your life.
AI Generation
Upload notes, PDFs, or slides. Gemini writes targeted Q&A flashcards grouped by lesson. Source material stays attached to every deck.
"Stop making cards. Start learning."
Busyness-Adaptive Scheduling
Set your weekly availability. FSRS calculates optimal timing — then our adaptive layer redistributes cards across free days. Busy Monday? Cards shift to Tuesday. No backlog, no guilt.
"Your schedule shapes your study — not the other way around."
Lesson-Based Review
Other apps shuffle cards from 12 subjects at once. Qvise groups reviews by source — Thursday is Cardiac Physiology, not a random queue. Sessions that match how memory actually works.
"Review by lesson. Not by random queue."
